## 1. Sovereign Credit Analysis

Sovereign credit analysis evaluates whether a government can generate stable growth, maintain low inflation, and service debt over time.

Unlike corporations, sovereigns rely on fiscal and monetary capacity rather than operating business cash flow.

---

## 2. Qualitative Factors

| Factor | Why it matters |
|------|------|
| **Fiscal flexibility** | Ability to raise taxes or reduce public spending |
| **Monetary effectiveness** | Ability of the central bank to manage money supply and inflation |
| **Political stability** | Policy consistency and willingness to repay |
| **Economic structure** | Diversification and resilience of the economy |
| **External position** | Dependence on foreign currency funding and imports |

Fiscal flexibility is stronger when a government has room to increase revenue or cut spending without causing severe economic damage.

---

## 3. Quantitative Factors

Common quantitative indicators:

| Indicator | Interpretation |
|------|------|
| Debt / GDP | Debt burden relative to economic output |
| Fiscal deficit / GDP | Ongoing borrowing need |
| Interest expense / revenue | Debt-service pressure |
| GDP growth | Capacity to expand tax base |
| Inflation | Monetary stability |
| Foreign reserves | Ability to meet external obligations |

Higher growth, lower inflation, and manageable debt-service ratios generally support stronger sovereign credit quality.

---

## 4. Regional and Municipal Governments

Regional governments include provinces, states, cities, and local authorities.

Two common municipal bond types:

| Bond type | Repayment source |
|------|------|
| **General Obligation Bond** | General taxing authority of the issuer |
| **Revenue Bond** | Cash flow from a specific project |

Revenue bonds often have higher credit risk because repayment depends on the success of a project or infrastructure asset.

---

## 5. Key Risks

| Risk | Description |
|------|------|
| Currency risk | Especially relevant for external debt |
| Refinancing risk | Government must roll over debt at acceptable rates |
| Political risk | Willingness to repay can change |
| Concentration risk | Economy depends heavily on a narrow sector or commodity |

### Exam Points

- Sovereign analysis is about both ability and willingness to repay.
- Debt in reserve currency can reduce funding pressure.
- External debt exposes the issuer to foreign currency risk.
- General obligation bonds are backed by taxing power; revenue bonds are backed by project revenue.
