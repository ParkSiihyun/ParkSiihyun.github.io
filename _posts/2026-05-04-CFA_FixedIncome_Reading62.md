## 1. Corporate Credit Analysis

Corporate credit analysis evaluates both:

| Dimension | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Probability of default** | Likelihood that the issuer fails to pay |
| **Loss given default** | Severity of loss if default occurs |

Analysts use both qualitative and quantitative factors.

---

## 2. Qualitative Factors

| Factor | Questions to ask |
|------|------|
| Business model | Is the revenue base stable and understandable? |
| Industry competition | Is the issuer protected from margin pressure? |
| Business risk | How cyclical or volatile are cash flows? |
| Corporate governance | Are debtholders treated fairly and legally? |
| Event risk | Could acquisitions, litigation, or restructuring weaken credit quality? |

---

## 3. Covenants

Covenants restrict issuer behavior to protect debtholders.

| Issuer type | Typical covenant pattern |
|------|------|
| Investment grade unsecured issuer | More affirmative covenants |
| High yield issuer | More negative covenants |

| Covenant type | Meaning |
|------|------|
| **Affirmative covenant** | Issuer must do something, such as provide financial statements |
| **Negative covenant** | Issuer is restricted from doing something, such as issuing more debt |

Analysts should assess whether management can issue additional debt that dilutes existing bondholder claims.

---

## 4. Quantitative Factors

Analysts estimate future financial statements and cash flows.

| Approach | Focus |
|------|------|
| **Top-down** | Macro cycle, industry size, market share, external shocks |
| **Bottom-up** | Issuer-specific revenue, cost, balance sheet, and cash flow drivers |

Key metrics:

| Metric | Use |
|------|------|
| EBITDA | Operating earnings before depreciation and amortization |
| CFO | Operating cash flow |
| FCF | Cash flow after capital expenditures |
| Leverage ratios | Debt burden |
| Coverage ratios | Ability to service interest and debt |

---

## 5. Seniority and Recovery

In default, debt seniority determines priority of claims.

| Rank | Recovery implication |
|------|------|
| Secured debt | Higher priority, usually higher recovery |
| Senior unsecured debt | General claim on issuer assets |
| Subordinated debt | Lower priority, lower recovery |
| Equity | Residual claim |

Strict priority is not always followed perfectly in practice because restructurings often involve negotiation.

---

## 6. Structural Subordination

Structural subordination occurs when debt at a parent company is effectively junior to debt at a subsidiary.

Reason:

- subsidiary debt is serviced before assets or cash can be upstreamed to the parent
- subsidiary covenants may restrict transfers to the parent

### Exam Points

- Corporate credit analysis combines PD and LGD.
- High yield bonds usually rely more heavily on restrictive covenants.
- Secured debt usually has higher recovery than unsecured debt.
- Structural subordination can weaken parent-company bondholder claims.
