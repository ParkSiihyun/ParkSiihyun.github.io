## Main Notes

## 1. Government Issuers

Government-related fixed income issuers include sovereign governments, regional governments, municipalities, and government-related agencies.

| Issuer type | Description |
|------|------|
| **Sovereign government** | National government debt issuer |
| **Non-sovereign government** | States, provinces, cities, counties, and public authorities |
| **Agency / Quasi-government issuer** | Entity created or supported by government to fund public services |

Government yields are important because market participants use them as benchmarks for measuring the credit risk of non-sovereign debt.

---

## 2. Sovereign Debt

**Sovereign debt** = debt issued by a national government.

| Type | Typical features |
|------|------|
| **Developed market sovereigns** | Stable and diversified economies, transparent fiscal policy, often reserve-currency debt |
| **Emerging market sovereigns** | Less stable policy environment, more concentrated economies, higher currency and funding risk |

### Domestic vs External Debt

| Type | Meaning | Main risk |
|------|------|------|
| **Domestic debt** | Issued in local currency and often held by domestic investors | Inflation / monetary policy risk |
| **External debt** | Issued in foreign currency and often held by foreign investors | Foreign currency funding risk |

External debt can be riskier for an emerging market sovereign because the government cannot freely create foreign currency to repay it.

---

## 3. Non-Sovereign Government Debt

Non-sovereign government debt is issued by public entities below the national level.

| Instrument | Repayment source |
|------|------|
| **General Obligation Bonds** | General taxing power of the issuer |
| **Revenue Bonds** | Revenue from a specific project or service |
| **Agency Bonds** | Cash flows or support from a government-related entity |

Revenue bonds usually carry more project-specific risk than general obligation bonds.

---

## 4. Government Bond Issuance

Corporate issuers often raise debt through underwritten offerings, while sovereign issuers commonly use regular public auctions.

### Public Auction

| Bid type | Description |
|------|------|
| **Noncompetitive bid** | Investor accepts the auction price; allocation is usually guaranteed up to a limit |
| **Competitive bid** | Investor specifies yield or price; these bids determine the issue price |

### Single-Price vs Multiple-Price Auction

| Auction type | Winning bidders pay |
|------|------|
| **Single-price auction** | The same price, usually based on the cutoff yield |
| **Multiple-price auction** | The price or yield they individually bid |

---

## 5. Primary Dealers and Secondary Market

Primary dealers participate directly in government securities auctions and help distribute securities to other dealers and investors.

After issuance, government securities usually trade in the secondary market through dealer or OTC markets.

### Exam Points

- Sovereign yields are the baseline for pricing many fixed income instruments.
- Domestic currency debt is generally less exposed to foreign currency repayment risk than external debt.
- General obligation bonds rely on taxing power; revenue bonds rely on project revenue.
- Competitive bids set auction pricing; noncompetitive bids accept the auction result.
